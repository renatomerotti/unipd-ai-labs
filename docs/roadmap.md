# Roadmap

The order the work happens in. What to actually *do* in each phase is
[`../specs/central-bank-game/tasks.md`](../specs/central-bank-game/tasks.md).

| Phase | You end with |
|---|---|
| 1 — it runs | A briefing page in your browser |
| 2 — the economy | A table of the ten quarters you inherited |
| 3 — it plays | A rate you can set, and a lag you can feel |
| 4 — it scores you | A chart, a score, and a rule to lose to |
| 5 — Beat the rule | A policy rule of your own, scored against Taylor's on the Rule lab page (Row 19) |
| Challenge 1 | A shared real-interest-rate exercise, then one change of your own, checked against your own criterion |
| Challenge 2 | An economic question, answered with a plan and a test — once Challenge 1 is done |

## Why this order

Phases 1 and 2 build the surface: something that runs, and something to look at.
Neither teaches economics. They exist so that Phase 3 has somewhere to put the
lesson.

**Phase 3 carries the lab.** It is the only phase where getting the
implementation subtly wrong produces an application that still works and teaches
nothing — which is why it has a test written specifically to catch that.

Phase 4 adds the chart and scoreboard; Phase 5 completes the five specified phases. If a
session runs short it can be cut without damaging what students came to learn,
because the record table already shows the inflation path numerically.

**Beat the rule is Phase 5.** Its task list builds a separate Rule lab page
(Row 19), where you change two numbers and compare your rule with Taylor’s
without changing your Economy game. It builds on Phase 4.

**Two challenges close the lab.** Challenge 1 (Row 20) starts with the shared
real-interest-rate exercise, then one change of your own;
Challenge 2 is a question about the economy, answered with the model.
[improve.md](improve.md) has both.

## Not in the roadmap

Anything beyond the two challenges. After the course, the direction is yours, and
the method in [improve.md](improve.md) still applies.
