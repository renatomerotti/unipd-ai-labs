# Mission

The Central Bank Game exists to make a policy lag something you have felt, not something you
have been told.

Monetary policy acts with delays. Here a student sets the **policy rate**,
the interest rate the central bank sets, and sees its demand effect a quarter
later. A fading food and energy surge can lower inflation even while
core inflation rises. Abrupt moves add financial stress; hardship drives
migration. Separating these effects is the economic lesson.

The application is deliberately small. It is not the point. The point is
learning to specify work precisely enough that an artificial intelligence (AI) assistant can build it —
and the simulation is what makes that worth doing.

## Principles

- **The lesson outranks the software.** If a feature does not serve the lag, the
  cost of disinflation, financial stress, or the rules-versus-discretion argument, it does not
  belong.
- **Every number must be checkable by hand.** A student who cannot verify the
  arithmetic cannot tell a correct implementation from a plausible wrong one.
- **Assume nothing.** Not Python, not AI, not economics beyond first year.
- **Nobody gets stranded.** A broken project or an exhausted request allowance
  must never end someone's course.
